# Shadecode Student: Competitive Landscape (4 November 2026)

## Why this exists
Prosperity Academics is one competitor, not the competitive set. Shadecode Student competes for student attention, trust, learning time, school relationships, and willingness to pay. This snapshot separates Zimbabwean direct competitors from broader African/global substitutes.

**Research caveat:** product capabilities and audience numbers below are taken from public product pages, app listings, or news reports. Most are self-reported and have not been independently audited. Treat claims as positioning signals, not verified performance.

## 2. Regional and global substitutes

These are not all direct syllabus competitors, but they compete for the same learning time and set expectations:

- **uLesson** — African curriculum-aligned video lessons and practice, with mobile-first distribution.
- **Khan Academy / Khanmigo** — free structured instruction, mastery practice and AI tutoring.
- **Quizlet** — flashcards, recall practice and AI-assisted study workflows.
- **Seneca Learning** — curriculum-linked retrieval practice and progress feedback.
- **Brilliant** — interactive maths, science and computer-science learning.
- **YouTube and WhatsApp study groups** — free explanations, peer support and resource sharing; often the strongest substitute because they are already habitual.

Regional ecosystem context: [uLesson competitor overview](https://todayafrica.co/inside-ulessons-journey/) describes African competitors including Eneza Education, M-Shule, Ubongo, FoondaMate and others.

## 3. What the market is telling us

1. **Past papers are table stakes.** Several local products already claim large question banks, explanations, timed exams and offline access.
2. **“AI tutor” is no longer differentiation.** Local products now advertise AI tutors, instant marking, adaptive quizzes, mark-scheme feedback and even offline AI.
3. **Offline is a battleground, not a moat by itself.** Danho, Khonnect/Amigo, ZEXA and others publicly claim offline capability. Shadecode must demonstrate what works offline, what syncs later, and what degrades gracefully.
4. **Curriculum coverage is crowded and expensive to maintain.** Competitors advertise ZIMSEC, Cambridge, multiple levels and dozens of subjects. We need visible syllabus-version coverage and editorial QA, not inflated “all subjects” claims.
5. **Practical learning is emerging as a differentiator.** Virtual labs and practical exam workflows are already appearing. Shadecode must connect practical tasks to evidence, feedback and real skill progression.
6. **Schools, teachers and parents are distribution channels.** Several products are building school directories, teacher tools, parent dashboards or institutional relationships.
7. **Trust is a product feature.** Accurate marking, provenance, privacy, reliable sync, honest coverage labels and transparent limitations matter more than feature-count marketing.

## 4. Shadecode's strategic position

Do not try to out-Danho Danho on raw question count, out-uLesson uLesson on video volume, or out-ZEXA ZEXA on a slogan about offline AI.

Build a **student learning operating system** that connects:

- **Cortex learning intelligence:** durable learner history, misconception patterns, confidence/uncertainty, spaced revisits and evidence-based next actions.
- **Learn:** structured, syllabus-versioned teaching with prerequisites, worked examples, retrieval practice and mastery evidence.
- **Exam intelligence:** authentic paper workflows, timed attempts, diagrams/figures, mark-scheme feedback, error classification and weak-topic follow-through.
- **Practical intelligence:** practical task completion, observation/measurement, method, safety, analysis and transferable skill evidence.
- **Student development:** study planning, focus, projects, communication, digital/technical skills and reflection.
- **Offline intelligence:** local content and saved attempts, robust recovery, queued sync, clear offline indicators and AI-optional workflows.

The defensible advantage is not six separate modules. It is the shared learner model: a lesson, exam error, practical task and revision session should improve the same understanding of what the learner can do and what they should do next.

## 5. Fast execution priorities

### P0: Trust and reliability (release blockers)
- Verify Android signed AAB, package metadata, API target, versioning and install path.
- Test account creation, sign-in, refresh, sign-out, recovery and account deletion end-to-end.
- Publish an accurate privacy policy and an accessible account-deletion request path; align Play Data safety declarations with actual SDKs, server providers and data flows.
- Test offline lesson/paper access, saved exam attempts, force-close recovery and reconnect/sync conflict handling.
- Fix crashes, broken navigation and data-loss bugs before adding novelty.

### P1: Make differentiation visible
- A “Why this next?” explanation for every Cortex recommendation, grounded in actual learner evidence.
- A single learner timeline across lessons, questions, exam attempts and practical tasks.
- Error-to-action loop: identify the misconception, assign a targeted explanation/practice, then verify improvement.
- Practical-skill evidence records and recommended next practical task.
- Explicit content provenance, syllabus version, review status and coverage gaps.

### P2: Win distribution
- Recruit a small cohort of real students across O-Level and A-Level, then measure activation, week-1 retention, completed learning loops and offline success.
- Get teacher review of selected lessons, worked solutions and practical tasks.
- Create school-ready demos and a simple teacher feedback channel.
- Use honest product pages and release notes. Do not publish unverified student counts, success rates or syllabus coverage.

## 6. Competitive metrics to track monthly

- First-session activation: learner completes profile + first meaningful learning action.
- Time to first value.
- Week-1 and week-4 retained learners.
- Lessons completed and mastery verified (not just opened).
- Practice attempts completed; error recurrence after remediation.
- Exam attempt completion and saved-answer recovery rate.
- Offline session success and sync conflict/loss rate.
- Practical tasks completed with evidence.
- Recommendation acceptance and subsequent improvement.
- Teacher/school referrals and cohort retention.
- Cost per active learner and AI cost per meaningful learning outcome.
