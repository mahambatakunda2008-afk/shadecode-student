# O-Level Geography Case Study Revision Kit

This kit helps a learner turn teacher-assigned case studies into short, exam-usable evidence. It is a revision structure, not a substitute for the learner's syllabus, teacher's list, textbook, or verified class notes.

## The workflow

1. **Collect the 24 assigned cases.** Copy the exact titles and topics from the teacher's list. Do not substitute cases just because they are easier to find online.
2. **Verify each case.** Use the class textbook/notes first, then reliable educational or official sources. Record source titles and URLs. Treat statistics, dates, locations, and named schemes as unverified until checked.
3. **Make one compact case card per case.** Use the template in [CASE_STUDY_TEMPLATE.md](./CASE_STUDY_TEMPLATE.md).
4. **Create retrieval flashcards.** Use [FLASHCARD_TEMPLATE.md](./FLASHCARD_TEMPLATE.md). Questions should prompt recall, not merely recognition.
5. **Practise exam application.** For each case, answer one short question using precise evidence and explain how that evidence supports the point.
6. **Review on a spaced schedule.** Review on the same day, the next day, three days later, one week later, and again before the test. Move difficult cards forward; do not mark a case mastered merely because it was read.

## Minimum standard for each case

A learner should be able to state, without notes:

- the named place and relevant scale;
- the issue, event, or process;
- at least two accurate causes or contributing factors where relevant;
- actions taken and who took them;
- outcomes, including limitations or unintended effects;
- two or three verified pieces of evidence (specific place names, dates, figures, or examples);
- how the case supports an answer to a command word such as *describe*, *explain*, *compare*, or *evaluate*.

Not every case fits every heading. Adapt the card to the topic instead of inventing an outcome or forcing irrelevant details.

## Online research and accuracy

Prefer the official exam-board syllabus and teacher-provided list, government/statistical publications, recognised educational institutions, reputable textbooks, and named organisations responsible for a scheme. Use videos as explanations, not as the sole source for statistics. Keep the source URL and the date checked. If a fact cannot be verified, mark it **CHECK** rather than guessing.

A generated card or flashcard is a draft until its factual claims have been checked. Never present invented statistics, quotations, dates, schemes, or outcomes as facts.

## Product direction

The intended learning loop is **source → concise notes → recall → exam application → feedback → targeted review**. Do not turn the experience into long narrative lessons or treat time spent reading as proof of mastery.
