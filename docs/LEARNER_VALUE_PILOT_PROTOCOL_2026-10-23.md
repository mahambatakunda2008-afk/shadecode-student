# Shadecode Student: small learner-value pilot protocol

**Protocol date:** 2026-10-23  
**Status:** Proposed protocol; no participants recruited and no outcomes collected by this document.  
**Purpose:** Find out whether one narrowly scoped Shadecode workflow is useful before making learning-efficacy claims.

## First question

For secondary learners preparing for Cambridge or ZIMSEC, does a Shadecode-guided lesson-and-practice session help them complete a useful study task and solve a fresh question independently better than their ordinary revision approach?

This pilot tests a workflow, not whether AI is inherently better than teachers, past papers or structured revision.

## Scope

- **Cohort:** 5–8 volunteers currently studying a supported Cambridge or ZIMSEC subject. Start with one subject and one verified topic. Do not recruit a mixed set of subjects until the first run is interpretable.
- **Duration:** Two short sessions per learner within 3–7 days, plus an optional delayed recall check.
- **Session A:** Learner uses their ordinary revision method for a fixed 20-minute study period.
- **Session B:** Learner uses the current Shadecode workflow for the same amount of time on a matched topic or matched question set. Counterbalance the order across learners where feasible to reduce order effects.
- **Assessment:** Each session ends with a short, previously unseen question set of comparable difficulty. Mark using a pre-written answer key or rubric, not the learner's impression of how well the tool worked.
- **Delayed check:** If practical, give a brief fresh-question check 2–7 days later without showing the answers first.

Do not run this protocol until the chosen topic, content source and marking rubric have been checked for correctness. If the two topic sets are not reasonably matched, report that limitation instead of treating score differences as causal evidence.

## What to record

Use a random participant code such as P01. Keep the contact/consent record separate from study observations.

Record only:
- participant code, broad stage and board/subject context needed to interpret the result;
- method used, minutes spent and whether the planned task was completed;
- pre/post question scores and marking rubric;
- number of hints/help requests if observable and useful;
- whether the learner could explain or apply the idea independently;
- reported friction or failure category, without collecting unnecessary personal narratives.

Do not collect account passwords, exact dates of birth, school names, personal academic records, private chat histories or unrelated personal details. Do not export individual learners' records into public reports.

## Consent and safeguarding

- Explain that this is an early product test, not tutoring or a guaranteed grade improvement.
- Participation is voluntary and can stop at any time without penalty.
- For participants under 18, obtain appropriate parent/guardian permission and the learner's own assent before collecting observations.
- Ask permission separately before recording a screen, voice, image or quotation. Default to no recording and no public quotation.
- Do not publish names, faces, schools or identifiable examples. Do not upload identifiable learner work to an AI provider for research without explicit, informed permission and a separate review of the data flow.
- Store the observation sheet in a restricted location and delete contact/consent details when no longer needed for the pilot's administration, subject to any applicable requirements.

## Measures and interpretation

Primary measures:
1. task completion within the fixed session;
2. independent score on fresh questions;
3. successful explanation/application without the system supplying the answer;
4. completion failures, generation failures and time lost to friction.

Secondary observations:
- learner preference and perceived usefulness;
- hint dependence or attempts to bypass guidance;
- content errors or feedback that misleads the learner.

Report counts and individual paired results descriptively. With 5–8 participants, the sample is too small to establish broad efficacy or represent all Zimbabwean learners. Do not claim statistical significance, causal improvement or general market demand. Preserve negative and mixed results.

## Stop conditions

Pause the session if:
- the content or marking key may be wrong;
- the learner's progress is being saved to the wrong account or cannot be recovered;
- another learner's data appears;
- an AI output is unsafe, humiliating or materially misleading;
- consent is unclear or the learner wants to stop.

Record the issue category without publishing private learner content. Fix critical privacy or data-integrity issues before continuing.

## Output after the pilot

Publish a short report containing the protocol, number invited/participating/completing, subject and board scope, task design, raw aggregate counts, known failures, limitations and next decision. Publish only aggregate data unless separately consented otherwise. If results are inconclusive or negative, say so and change the product or the hypothesis rather than polishing the narrative.
