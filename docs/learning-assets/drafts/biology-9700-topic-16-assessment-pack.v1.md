## Question 1: Meiosis, chromosome behaviour and microscopy (10 marks)

A diploid organism has a diploid chromosome number of 2n = 6. A cell undergoes meiosis.

**(a)** State the number of chromosomes and chromatids in the nucleus at metaphase I, after DNA replication has occurred. (2)

**(b)** State the number of chromosomes in each daughter cell immediately after meiosis I. Explain why each chromosome still consists of two chromatids. (2)

**(c)** State the number of chromosomes present in one cell at metaphase II and the number present in that cell at anaphase II after the centromeres have divided. (2)

**(d)** Explain how crossing over can produce genetically different chromatids. (2)

**(e)** A student identifies a cell as being in anaphase I because homologous chromosomes are moving towards opposite poles. Give one visible feature that would help distinguish anaphase I from anaphase II in a suitable micrograph. (2)

### Mark guidance

- **(a)** 6 chromosomes (1); 12 chromatids (1).
- **(b)** 3 chromosomes per daughter cell (1); sister chromatids have not separated in meiosis I, so each chromosome remains duplicated / consists of two chromatids (1).
- **(c)** Metaphase II: 3 chromosomes in each cell (1); anaphase II: 6 chromosomes in that cell once centromeres divide and sister chromatids become individual chromosomes (1). Accept clear explanation that three move towards each pole.
- **(d)** Exchange of corresponding sections between non-sister chromatids of homologous chromosomes (1); produces new combinations of alleles / recombinant chromatids (1).
- **(e)** Anaphase I: homologous chromosomes, each still with two chromatids, move apart (1); anaphase II: sister chromatids separate, with individual chromatids moving to opposite poles (1).

**Examiner-style note:** In (c), the question deliberately asks for the chromosome count *in one cell*. At anaphase II, centromere division means the separated chromatids are counted as individual chromosomes until cytokinesis. Do not confuse chromosome number with DNA molecule or chromatid number.

## Question 2: Linked genes and interpretation of offspring data (8 marks)

A plant heterozygous for two linked genes is test-crossed with an individual homozygous recessive for both genes. The offspring are:

| Offspring phenotype | Number |
|---|---:|
| AB | 412 |
| ab | 398 |
| Ab | 96 |
| aB | 94 |
| **Total** | **1000** |

Assume the offspring classes can be distinguished reliably.

**(a)** Identify the parental and recombinant phenotype classes. Explain your choice. (2)

**(b)** Calculate the recombination frequency. Show your working. (2)

**(c)** Explain why the recombinant classes are less frequent than the parental classes. (2)

**(d)** State one reason why the calculated recombination frequency should not automatically be treated as an exact physical distance between the genes. (2)

### Mark guidance

- **(a)** Parental: AB and ab (1); they are the two most frequent classes, while Ab and aB are recombinant (1).
- **(b)** Recombinants = 96 + 94 = 190 (1); recombination frequency = 190 / 1000 × 100 = **19%** (1).
- **(c)** The genes are on the same chromosome and tend to be inherited together (1); crossing over between the loci produces recombinant chromatids / gametes (1).
- **(d)** Recombination frequency is an estimate from a finite sample and is subject to sampling variation (1); multiple crossovers can restore parental allele arrangements / recombination frequency does not increase linearly with physical distance over larger intervals (1).

**Data-handling check:** The total is 1000. The recombination frequency is 19%, not 190%, and the two largest classes are treated as parental under the stated test-cross interpretation.

## Question 3: Gene control and experimental evaluation (7 marks)

A student investigates stem elongation in seedlings over 48 hours. The student records the following mean values:

| Treatment | Mean stem elongation |
|---|---:|
| No added gibberellin | 4 mm |
| Gibberellin | 18 mm |
| Gibberellin + treatment that prevents DELLA breakdown | 5 mm |

**(a)** Calculate the percentage increase in mean stem elongation in the gibberellin group compared with the untreated group. Show your working. (2)

**(b)** Explain why preventing DELLA breakdown reduces the stem-elongation response to gibberellin. (3)

**(c)** Give two limitations of using stem elongation alone as evidence for the proposed molecular mechanism. (2)

### Mark guidance

- **(a)** Increase = 18 − 4 = 14 mm (1); percentage increase = 14 / 4 × 100 = **350%** (1).
- **(b)** Gibberellin signalling normally promotes DELLA breakdown (1); DELLA proteins inhibit / repress transcription factors (1); if DELLA breakdown is prevented, transcription factors remain inhibited and transcription of growth-related target genes is reduced (1).
- **(c)** Any two distinct points: stem elongation is an indirect whole-organism measurement; it does not directly measure DELLA abundance; it does not directly measure transcription-factor activity or target-gene transcription; other pathways or treatment effects could influence growth; further controls and molecular measurements are needed. (1 each)

**Interpretation caution:** The data are consistent with the proposed pathway but do not, by themselves, prove that DELLA is the only mediator or identify the exact molecular events.

## Question 4: Test-cross design and inference (5 marks)

A plant has purple flowers. Purple is dominant to white. The plant could therefore be homozygous dominant or heterozygous. A student wants to determine its genotype.

**(a)** State the genotype of the tester plant that should be used and explain why. (2)

**(b)** Give the expected offspring phenotypes if the purple-flowered plant is homozygous dominant, and if it is heterozygous. (2)

**(c)** Explain why a small offspring sample in which all plants are purple does not prove that the unknown parent is homozygous dominant. (1)

### Mark guidance

- **(a)** Homozygous recessive, pp (1); it contributes only recessive alleles, so offspring phenotypes reveal whether the unknown parent can transmit p (1).
- **(b)** Unknown PP × pp: all offspring Pp and purple (1); unknown Pp × pp: expected 1 purple : 1 white (1).
- **(c)** A heterozygous parent could, by chance, produce only purple offspring in a small sample / a larger sample gives a more reliable estimate (1).

## Question 5: Epistasis and chi-squared interpretation (8 marks)

In a plant, pigment production requires a functional enzyme coded for by gene P. A second gene, C, is needed for the pigment pathway to operate. Plants with genotype cc are white, regardless of their genotype at the P locus. The genes assort independently.

**(a)** Explain why this is an example of epistasis. (2)

**(b)** A cross PpCc × PpCc is carried out. The proposed model predicts a 9:3:4 ratio of coloured : unpigmented : white offspring, corresponding to the expected numbers in the table. This ratio is supplied as context; candidates are not expected to memorise epistasis ratios. Identify the genotype classes that produce each phenotype. (2)

A sample of 160 offspring gives the following results:

| Phenotype | Observed number | Expected number |
|---|---:|---:|
| Coloured | 84 | 90 |
| Unpigmented | 36 | 30 |
| White | 40 | 40 |
| **Total** | **160** | **160** |

**(c)** Calculate the chi-squared value. Show your working. (2)

**(d)** The critical value at p = 0.05 and 2 degrees of freedom is 5.99. State the statistical decision and explain what it means. (2)

### Mark guidance

- **(a)** The cc genotype masks the effect of the P gene (1); the interaction is between two different gene loci (1).
- **(b)** P_C_ = coloured, ppC_ = unpigmented, and __cc = white (2). The 9:3:4 model is supplied in the question and is not a ratio candidates are expected to memorise.
- **(c)** χ² = (84−90)²/90 + (36−30)²/30 + (40−40)²/40 (1); χ² = 36/90 + 36/30 + 0 = **1.6** (1).
- **(d)** 1.6 is below 5.99, so the difference is not statistically significant at p = 0.05 (1); do not reject the null hypothesis, and do not claim that the genetic model has been proved (1).

**Interpretation check:** There are three phenotype categories, so degrees of freedom = 3 − 1 = 2 for this goodness-of-fit test. The result is consistent with the expected frequencies supplied in the question, but it does not prove the model is true.
