# Curriculum resource register

Last researched: 2026-10-29. This is a source register, not a claim that Shadecode Student has completed syllabus mapping or obtained redistribution rights.

## Authority order

1. **Current official syllabus and assessment documents** for the exact board, qualification, subject code, examination year and component.
2. Official specimen papers, publicly available past papers, mark schemes and examiner reports for that same syllabus and permitted session.
3. Official published/endorsed-resource lists and the relevant books/editions.
4. Reputable open educational resources (OER), teacher-created material and simulations, with item-level licensing checks.
5. Original Shadecode explanations, diagrams, worked examples and practice tasks, mapped back to the official syllabus and reviewed independently.

When sources conflict, the applicable official syllabus and examination documentation govern. Never infer a syllabus from a book alone, and never treat a search result or a third-party mirror as proof that a document is current.

## Rights rules for Shadecode Student

Shadecode may eventually use sponsorships, school licensing or other revenue. Therefore, “free to read” does **not** mean “free to redistribute inside our product.”

- Store content only if it is original, public domain, explicitly licensed for the intended use, or covered by documented permission.
- Treat non-commercial licences as incompatible with commercial/ad-supported distribution until permission or legal review confirms otherwise.
- Keep a per-asset record: title, creator, source URL, version, retrieval date, licence name and URL, attribution text, modifications, redistribution decision, and reviewer.
- Do not scrape or bundle copyrighted textbooks, publisher question banks, past-paper scans, mark schemes, answer keys or diagrams wholesale.
- Prefer official links for restricted or copyrighted resources. Allow users to import files they are entitled to use, subject to applicable terms.
- Create original lessons, examples, diagrams, exam-style questions and marking guidance. Use official documents to establish scope and assessment expectations, not as text to copy.
- Store only the minimum source excerpt needed for private analysis during authoring. The shipped learning content should be independently written and traceable to its source map.

## Syllabus inventory workflow

For every distinct board + qualification + subject code + examination year:

1. Open the official syllabus and record its title, code, examination years, version/publication date, URL and date checked.
2. Extract every section, topic and subtopic into a stable ledger. Preserve official numbering where present; assign stable internal IDs so later syllabus revisions can be compared.
3. Record assessment objectives, paper/component codes, weighting, permitted materials, practical/coursework/oral requirements and any variant-specific differences.
4. Connect each topic to resources, authored lessons, worked examples, visual/practical activities, original practice questions, checked answers and offline-pack status.
5. Compare the inventory with the previous syllabus version and flag additions, removals and changed assessment requirements.
6. Independently review subject accuracy and syllabus mapping before marking any row verified.
7. Recheck source links and applicable syllabus years on a scheduled basis and whenever the exam board announces a change.

## Coverage status vocabulary

- `not-started`: official source not yet inventoried.
- `inventoried`: source/version captured; topic list not yet fully atomised.
- `mapped`: all official topic/subtopic entries are represented in the ledger.
- `authored`: original lesson/practice exists for the mapped item.
- `content-checked`: calculations, factual claims, examples and answers independently checked.
- `syllabus-checked`: checked against the exact official syllabus/version and assessment expectations.
- `offline-ready`: required assets are legally distributable, packaged, versioned and integrity-checked for offline use.
- `verified`: content, syllabus mapping, assessment coverage and required review are complete.
- `blocked-rights`: useful source exists but redistribution rights are absent or unclear; use a link or replace with original material.

A subject is not “comprehensive” because it has many lessons. It is comprehensive only when every applicable official syllabus item is accounted for and the evidence for its coverage is visible.
