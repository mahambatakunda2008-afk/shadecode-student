## Accuracy and scope notes

- Continuous traits are treated as commonly polygenic and environmentally influenced; discontinuous traits are commonly controlled by one or a few genes.
- The t-test compares sample means. The formula is provided in the examination; learners must identify the null hypothesis and interpret significance against the critical value.
- Natural selection changes populations through differential reproductive success; it does not involve organisms intentionally adapting.
- Genetic drift is random and especially influential in small populations. Founder and bottleneck effects can reduce variation.
- Antibiotic resistance is selected from pre-existing genetic variation; antibiotic exposure does not cause bacteria to mutate in a directed way.
- Hardy–Weinberg equations use p + q = 1 and p² + 2pq + q² = 1. The package states the equilibrium assumptions and avoids claiming that a non-significant deviation proves equilibrium.
- Artificial selection examples match the syllabus: disease resistance in wheat and rice, inbreeding/hybridisation in maize, and milk yield in dairy cattle.
- Diagrams are explanatory schematics, not experimental data or measured distributions.
