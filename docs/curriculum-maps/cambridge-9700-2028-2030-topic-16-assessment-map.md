## Accuracy and scope notes

- Meiosis is described as one DNA replication followed by two divisions. Homologues separate in meiosis I; sister chromatids separate in meiosis II.
- Crossing over occurs between non-sister chromatids of homologous chromosomes. Random orientation at metaphase I and II contributes to independent assortment.
- A 9:3:3:1 ratio is only expected for two independently assorting genes with complete dominance at both loci.
- Linked genes produce excess parental-type combinations; crossing over produces recombinant combinations. Epistasis ratios are not required to be memorised.
- Chi-squared interpretation uses the stated significance threshold correctly: a value above the critical value is significant; a value at or below it is not significant. A non-significant result does not prove the null hypothesis.
- The TYR, HBB, F8 and HTT sections connect DNA variation to protein function and phenotype. The sickle-cell explanation includes oxygen-dependent HbS polymerisation.
- Le is treated as a functional gibberellin synthesis allele and le as non-functional, in line with the syllabus model.
- The lac operon explanation focuses on repressor/operator control and inducer action; detailed cAMP regulation is not taught as a required outcome here.
- The diagrams are original explanatory schematics, not authentic photomicrographs. Learners need practice identifying stages in real microscope images.
