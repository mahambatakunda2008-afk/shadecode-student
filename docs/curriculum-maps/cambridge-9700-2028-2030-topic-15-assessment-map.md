## Accuracy and scope notes

- Action-potential graph is schematic; membrane potentials and timing are illustrative, not measured data.
- Resting potential is explained through selective membrane permeability, ion gradients and the sodium-potassium pump. The pump maintains gradients; it is not presented as the sole direct cause of the resting potential.
- Taste receptor cells and sensory neurones are distinguished. The taste-bud example uses receptor-cell depolarisation and neurotransmitter-mediated stimulation of the sensory neurone.
- The refractory period limits impulse frequency; stronger stimuli are represented by higher frequency, not larger action-potential amplitude.
- The synapse explanation includes calcium entry, vesicle fusion, acetylcholine, postsynaptic receptors, acetylcholinesterase and choline recycling.
- Muscle diagrams are explanatory schematics, not authentic electron micrographs. Learners still need practice interpreting real electron micrographs and drawing biological diagrams.
- The sliding-filament account distinguishes the A band (unchanged width) from the I band and H zone (narrow during contraction). ATP is required for myosin detachment and re-cocking.
- The Venus flytrap explanation avoids implying that plants have muscles; closure is described through electrical signalling and changes in leaf-lobe mechanics.
- Auxin-induced elongation is described through proton pumping, lower cell-wall pH, wall loosening and turgor-driven extension.
- Gibberellin in barley is linked to aleurone alpha-amylase production and mobilisation of endosperm starch.
- Questions are original learning checks, not official Cambridge questions or mark schemes.
