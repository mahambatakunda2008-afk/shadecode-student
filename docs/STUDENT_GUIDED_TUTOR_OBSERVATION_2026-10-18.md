# Shadecode Student Guided Tutor Observation Protocol

**Date:** 2026-10-18  
**Status:** Formative discovery guide. Not a validated research instrument.

## Purpose

Observe whether real students can get useful help from the existing tutor without being coached through the interface. Learn where guidance feels helpful, slow, confusing, or restrictive. This is not a test of the student.

## Participants and safeguards

- Observe five students who resemble the intended users, with a mix of subjects and confidence levels where practical.
- Ask permission before starting. Explain that the product is being tested, not the participant, and they may stop at any time.
- Do not collect names, school identifiers, grades, or recordings unless there is a clear need and explicit permission. Anonymous notes are enough.
- Do not use the session to claim learning efficacy, grade improvement, or market validation. Five sessions reveal usability problems and hypotheses only.

## Moderator script

Say only:

> “Please use Shadecode Student to work on a topic or question you would genuinely want help with. Use it however you normally would. I may take a few anonymous notes about the experience. I won't explain how the tutor is supposed to work, because we want to see what feels natural to you.”

Then stay quiet. If the student asks how to use a feature, say: “What would you expect to happen if you tried it?” Do not explain the Socratic design or point out help buttons unless the participant discovers them independently.

## Suggested 10–15 minute session

1. Ask the participant to choose a real topic/question they want to understand.
2. Observe their first prompt and the tutor's first response.
3. Let them continue naturally, including asking for direct answers if that is what they want.
4. If they reach a useful explanation, ask them to solve a similar question without the tutor. Do not coach the solution.
5. Ask at the end: “What was most useful?”, “Where did it waste your time?”, and “Would you use it again for a real study task? Why or why not?”

## Decision rules after five sessions

- Prioritize repeated friction observed independently by at least two participants before adding more features.
- If students ask for answers directly, inspect whether they are stuck, under time pressure, already understand the concept, or simply prefer another format. Do not assume all direct requests mean the same thing.
- If students miss the help actions, improve their discoverability and wording before adding more controls.
- If students like the explanation but fail the independent follow-up, improve teaching and practice transfer rather than presenting conversation length as progress.
- If students cannot get a useful response quickly, fix the time-to-useful-help path before investing in cosmetic polish.
- Record contradictory observations as well as confirming ones.

## Outcome metrics to track over time

These are separate measures, not a single engagement score:

- **Time to useful help:** seconds until the student says or shows they can take the next step.
- **Resolution:** whether the student completes the task or can explain a valid next step.
- **Transfer:** whether the student can solve a related item without tutor help.
- **Agency:** whether they can change explanation style or ask for more help when needed.
- **Return intent:** whether they would use it for another genuine task, and why.

Do not advertise score increases until there is appropriate longitudinal evidence comparing a student's performance under a consistent assessment method. A product can promise useful practice and clearer feedback; it cannot guarantee grades.
